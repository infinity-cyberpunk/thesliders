Flow

Their feelings rise slowly, inexorably — like water filling a vessel. Every drop brings closer the moment when everything overflows: the silence, the glances, the unspoken words. Nika and Vika both know: one movement, one sound, and the fragile balance will shatter. But what will happen when the flow finally breaks free? And can anything stop it?

CHAPTER FOUR. UNDER CONTROL

I sat in the dark corner, far from the window. The light barely reached here, and only the cold glow of the monitor outlined the contours of our faces. Beside me, slightly to the right, on the neighbouring chair — Nika. We were looking at the screen: a new project required selecting models from a lingerie catalogue.

Nika commented eagerly on every photo. With the scroll wheel she zoomed the frame in and out, flipping through page after page. I stayed silent. I sat on the left, half-turned toward the screen, trying not to interrupt the process that seemed to have genuinely absorbed her.

“Look at this hairstyle,” she said suddenly, leaning closer, “it’s like yours.”

Her perfume wrapped around us both — something warm, slightly sweet, with a note I couldn’t identify. When she leaned in, the scent thickened, and I caught myself holding my breath to avoid inhaling it too obviously.

“That’s it. These ones will do,” — she clicked the mouse and selected several files. “We’ll find more options later.”

And abruptly, without transition, she changed the subject.

“Have you noticed my new toenail designs?”

I glanced at her hands — the same fingers, the same manicure as that morning. Nothing new. Catching my glance, she shook her head.

“On my feet.”

She turned her chair toward me. I looked down, under the desk. But after the bright glow of the screen we had both grown used to over the past hour, there under the desk I couldn’t make out anything at all. Darkness. My eyes hadn’t adapted yet.

“It’s dark,” I said. “I can’t see anything.”

Nika tried to lift her foot, but the desktop got in the way — the space was too narrow, the tabletop too low.

“Let’s go to the window,” she decided, and she was the first to slip off her chair, heading toward the light.

I followed her.

The desk stood parallel to the wall, right under the window. On the left, like a partition separating this spot from the rest of the office, stood a cabinet. In front of the desk — a chair. Nika sat down on it, her legs slightly apart. The sun from the window flooded everything around, and now her open-toe sandals, her miniature toenails, were perfectly lit.

I bent down. Her hair gave off that scent again, but a different one — shampoo, something with jasmine, soft and flowing. On her toenails — tiny little flowers, small, neat.

“They’re really tiny,” I said, examining them.

“Just a moment,” Nika replied, and lifted her left foot. The hem of her dress slid up her thigh and almost immediately dropped back down. “It’s hard to hold it like this. Squat down, you’ll see better.”

I crouched down in front of her. I was wearing a miniskirt, and in this position it had ridden up. I noticed Nika’s glance slide there, linger — but she said nothing.

These were not just flowers. Each one was painted in its own shade: tender pink, pale yellow, blue, lilac. And at the centre of each — a tiny stone that caught the light and shimmered.

“Here’s the pink one,” she pointed right in front of me, “this is the yellow one, and this is the blue. And look, the stem itself — it’s a gradient.”

I examined each flower. The work was painstaking, fanatical. Something like this must take hours.

“Each pattern has its own,” she explained, looking at her nails. “There — I’ve got it!”

She kept her right foot further from the chair, and slipped the open-toe sandal off her left one. She pulled the hem of her dress higher and placed her foot on the seat of the chair. Her toes moved.

“Is this more comfortable? Come closer!”

I moved closer. My head ended up very close to her right thigh. The scent — no longer jasmine, but something fruity, peach. And under the lifted fabric of her dress I could see white lace panties, sheer and revealing, semi-transparent.

“They really are beautiful,” I answered, returning my gaze to the designs.

She kept talking about the patterns, the shades, about how long she had been choosing the little stones. I nodded along, pretended to admire, kept nodding. But her foot lay on the seat right next to her panties, and examining her toenails, I inevitably saw them too. The fabric, and the lace, and what was beneath it.

*What am I doing?* I thought. *What if someone walks in? And here I am, crouched in front of Nika, staring under the hem of her dress.*

At that moment a strand of my hair fell forward. Nika caught it with her fingers, leaned toward me — close, very close — and tucked the strand behind my ear. Holding her hand there for half a second. I felt the warmth of her fingers and her breath on the skin at my temple.

“Now, I’ll show you the other one. Look carefully.”

And before lowering her foot, she lifted the dress even higher with one hand, and with the other — adjusted her panties. I saw a few stray hairs. I tore my gaze away with difficulty.

*Any moment now someone will walk in and see what we’re doing here. If only the phone would ring. If only something would distract us.*

But Nika had already lowered that foot and placed the other one on the seat. “Here it’s a completely different design. Can you see it well?”

I nodded. I couldn’t say anything.

Triangles and rhombuses, woven together in a pattern like an oriental carpet. Each geometric element filled with a gradient — from one shade into another. And on every triangle, on every rhombus — a little rhinestone.

My legs were throbbing from the strain.

“Magnificent,” I praised her, because it truly was beautifully done. “And how long did they take you?”

“Oh, a long time. Two evenings! But it’s lovely, isn’t it?”

“Of course.”

“And would you like some like these?” she asked, looking down at me from above. “I can do them for you too. I have all the polishes, the rhinestones — everything.”

I was looking at the triangles and rhombuses right in front of my face. At the glitter of the rhinestones. At her foot, which lay on the chair half a centimetre from my shoulder.

“Will you come over to my place?”

My muscles were trembling. My legs had gone completely numb. I knew that if I stood up now, they would give way beneath me. But I needed to stand up — right now, while I still could. I braced my hand on the seat of the chair, accidentally touching her foot, and flinched.

And at that moment she leans forward and rises — at the same time I do. And now we are standing face to face, close, almost touching. I needed to step away. Take a step back. Just one step.

But I can’t.

“Will you come over, Vik?” she asks again. Very quietly, looking straight into my eyes.

I look at the door. Listening. No footsteps. Silence. The air conditioner hums.

“N-no,” I say.

“Or maybe on Saturday or Sunday?”

“It won’t work out.”

But that wasn’t an answer. That was a lie, and we both knew it.

Nika smiled slightly. As if it amused her — my refusal, my awkwardness, my inability to tell the truth. As if it were part of a game whose rules only she knew.

“All right,” she said.

And she went back to her desk.

I stayed standing by the window, looking out at the street. Below, people were going about their business. No one knew what had just happened. In broad daylight, with the door open.

My fingers were trembling.
